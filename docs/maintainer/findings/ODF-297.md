### Implemented response — 2026-10-21

`resolve-dependency --correct-agreement` now applies an explicitly reviewed,
proved correction to the same existing supplier relationship using recoverable
historical completion and accepted integration. It keeps the current dependency
basis guard, refuses introduction or retargeting, and retains the original
resolution locator and summary when adding the new condition judgment and its
validated evidence. Ordinary live-home authoring and unchanged historical
resolution retain their existing behavior. The shared supplier procedure owns
the semantic judgment and stops for unsupported outcomes or an unresolved human
scope choice; the command does not interpret arbitrary condition prose.

Verified examples use isolated repositories with bare local origins, not
Pygardon's working copy or remote:

- A waiting mixed condition and an already-satisfied mixed condition are
  corrected after supplier source/plan cleanup. Original accepted evidence is
  retained, an exact replay is byte-stable, future consumption remains consumer
  work, and another unresolved supplier remains the only dependency blocker
  through the real execution-start command.
- Stale input, wrong or missing historical identity/home, incomplete or
  unaccepted completion, wrong evidence path, absent/retargeted relationship,
  missing proof and an unresolved correction judgment write nothing. The
  existing readiness judgment and unrelated Not ready reason remain recorded;
  the changed agreement reports changed content since review.
- A representative review recovered a whole-second retry endpoint outcome.
  Fractional-second consumer requirements remained waiting for missing supplier
  behavior/proof; an unspecified legacy-compatibility promise remained waiting
  for the developer's scope decision. Neither agreement was corrected. These
  were agent judgments from the supplier and consumer context, not keyword tests.
